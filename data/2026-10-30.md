Abstract: Understanding the Universe's large-scale distribution of dark matter is a central objective in the era of precision cosmology. A key technique for the study of dark matter is weak gravitational lensing: a phenomenon where light from distant galaxies is sheared as it passes through the gravitational field of a massive object, like a galaxy cluster. This shear, which manifests as a slight (weak) distortion of shapes over thousands of galaxies, allows astrophysicists to infer the distribution of total matter, including both luminous and dark matter. Obtaining a mass distribution from a catalog of galaxy shears involves reconstructing the convergence ($κ$), the projected surface mass density divided by the critical surface density for the lens-source system. The Shear Mapping in Python (SMPy) package provides a standardized, well-documented, and open-source solution for creating convergence maps from weak lensing galaxy shear measurements. SMPy implements three mapping methods: the classic Kaiser-Squires inversion, aperture mass mapping, and, to our knowledge, the first publicly available implementation of the full KS+ algorithm, which corrects for missing data, field borders, and reduced shear. SMPy was initially developed to support the Superpressure Balloon-borne Imaging Telescope (SuperBIT) and has since evolved into a general-purpose tool suitable for analyzing the weak lensing data from cosmological surveys.

Abstract: Intrinsic alignments (IA) of galaxies are an important source of systematic uncertainty in weak gravitational lensing measurements and also serve as a test for models of galaxy formation and evolution. These galaxy alignments are usually modeled assuming that the IA are sourced by the tidal fields around the galaxies. However, galaxy properties, like their proximity to other galaxies and their mass, provide alignment contributions beyond the tidal field. In this work, we perform novel model-agnostic tests to determine the impact of density-weighting on the IA of galaxies using data from the IllustrisTNG cosmological hydrodynamical simulation and SDSS-III (BOSS). We show that the primary effect of density-weighting is an increase of the alignment amplitude at large scales, whereas the small-scale impact is sample-dependent. We also assess the tidal alignment paradigm for IllustrisTNG galaxies using two-point correlation functions of galaxy shapes, tidal fields, and the sampled background tidal field of the simulation box. We find, given the precision of our measurements, that central galaxies align linearly to the underlying (non-linear) tidal field well into the 1-halo regime.

Abstract: The magnetic Prandtl number ($\mathrm{Pm}$), the ratio of viscosity to magnetic diffusivity, spans many orders of magnitude in astrophysical plasmas. It shapes how turbulence amplifies and structures magnetic fields. In direct numerical simulations (DNS) the viscosity and magnetic diffusivity can be controlled explicitly and independently, whereas in implicit large eddy simulations (ILES) they are set by the numerical scheme. We test whether, in a finite-volume code, $\mathrm{Pm}$ can be varied through the reconstruction scheme by applying different methods to the hydro and magnetic variables. As a proof of concept, we simulate driven, subsonic, homogeneous magnetohydrodynamic turbulence using five pairs of hydrodynamic and magnetic reconstruction schemes at varying resolutions. We measure the effective viscosity and resistivity from the energy transfer between scales. Reconstructing the magnetic field at higher order than the fluid raises the effective magnetic Prandtl number and vice versa. We demonstrate that a mix of second- and third-order reconstruction alone results in a $\gtrsim20\times$ change in $\mathrm{Pm}$. Mixed reconstruction thus offers a simple and efficient way to explore $\mathrm{Pm} \not\simeq 1$ in ILES without adding explicit dissipation.
